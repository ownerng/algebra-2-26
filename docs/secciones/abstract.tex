\section*{Abstract}

Building a three-dimensional model of a real object usually requires expensive
scanners or dozens of photographs and long processing times. This work
presents a low-cost 3D mapper that turns a single photograph taken with a
phone camera into a closed solid in about one second on a laptop. The object
is separated from the background without a neutral backdrop; the depth of
each pixel is estimated from its silhouette, and the inverse of the camera
matrix, $K^{-1}$, maps each pixel $(u,v)$ to a point $(X,Y,Z)$ in space. These
points form a triangle mesh, stored as a vertex matrix
$V\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times3}$ and a face matrix $F\in\mathbb{Z}^{M\times3}$, closed
with a back face and side walls. With five photographs (front, sides, back and
top), each view is described by a rotation matrix and the object is carved as
the intersection of the cones defined by its silhouettes. The whole process
relies on vectors, matrices and systems of linear equations. In our tests a
photograph is processed in 0.7 to 1.2 seconds and, with five views, the
proportions of a test box are recovered with less than 3\,\% error.

\textit{Keywords:} linear algebra, camera model, matrix inverse, 3D
reconstruction, triangle mesh, visual hull.
